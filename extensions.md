# Extension assessment: higher dimensions and general covariance

## Bottom line

My current view is:

- Extending the characterization to $k>2$ with $\Sigma=I_k$ is plausible.
- Extending it to arbitrary positive-definite $\Sigma$ is more difficult. Full rank alone does not preserve the geometry used by the bivariate identity-covariance proof.

## 1. Extending to $k>2$ with $\Sigma=I_k$

Suppose

$$
\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\cdots\ge\mu_k,
\qquad
\Lambda_1=\{\mu:\mu_1>0\}.
$$

Write

$$
\mu_j=u-\sum_{r=2}^j v_r,
\qquad
u>0,\quad v_r\ge0.
$$

Then

$$
\mu'x
=
u\sum_{j=1}^k x_j
+
\sum_{r=2}^k v_r\left(-\sum_{j=r}^k x_j\right).
$$

Thus the likelihood-ratio order is generated by

$$
T_1(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k x_j,
\qquad
T_r(x)=-\sum_{j=r}^k x_j,\quad r=2,\ldots,k.
$$

The corresponding sample-space cone is

$$
\Pi_k
=
\operatorname{cone}
\{e_1,e_1-e_2,\ldots,e_{k-1}-e_k\}.
$$

Its feasible normal cone, after imposing coordinatewise lowerness, is

$$
\mathcal F_k
=
\{a:a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_k\ge0\},
$$

generated by the prefix vectors

$$
(1,0,\ldots,0),\quad
(1,1,0,\ldots,0),\quad\ldots,\quad
(1,\ldots,1).
$$

Several parts of the bivariate proof extend almost directly:

- Lehmann--Romano sufficiency;
- the hull $\widehat A=\operatorname{cl}(A-\Pi_k)$;
- the fact that $\widehat A$ has only feasible normals;
- calibration by intersecting with
  $$
  \left\{\sum_{j=1}^k x_j\le b\right\}.
  $$

### Main bottleneck: necessity

In two dimensions there is only one weak-order coordinate, $T_2=-x_2$, and concavity produces a scalar separation point $s_+$. For $k>2$, gains and losses must be compared simultaneously in $k-1$ tail sums. They can be incomparable in that product order, so the one-dimensional slice argument does not carry over directly.

The needed result is probably a monotone-coupling theorem: show that the null mass removed from $A$ can be coupled with the added mass so that every transfer moves upward in all the $T_r$'s. A sequential adjacent-transfer or pool-adjacent-violators construction may work. A single total-sum cutoff, however, may be insufficient.

The higher-dimensional result also need not produce strict improvement at every alternative. Weak improvement everywhere and strict improvement somewhere is enough for inadmissibility. That relaxation may be useful.

## 2. General positive-definite $\Sigma$

Sufficiency still appears manageable. Let $D\mu\ge0$ encode

$$
\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_k.
$$

The natural feasible-normal cone becomes

$$
\mathcal F_\Sigma
=
\{a\ge0:D\Sigma a\ge0\}.
$$

For nonzero $a$ in this cone, $\Sigma a$ automatically has a positive first coordinate. Otherwise, the ordering restrictions would imply $\Sigma a\le0$, contradicting

$$
a'\Sigma a>0.
$$

Hence the Lehmann--Romano ray argument should extend.

### Main bottleneck: incompatibility of orders

The strictly positive diagonal-alternative score is

$$
T_1(x)=(\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1)'x.
$$

The natural calibration cut

$$
(\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1)'x\le b
$$

need not be coordinatewise lower because $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$ can have negative components. The resulting competitor may therefore lose level control at $\mu=0$.

This is not a remote pathology. Consider

$$
\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1.5\\
1.5&3
\end{pmatrix},
$$

which is positive definite. In this case,

$$
\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1=(2,-2/3)'.
$$

Moreover,

$$
(\Sigma a)_1-(\Sigma a)_2
=-\tfrac12a_1-\tfrac32a_2<0
$$

for every nonzero $a\ge0$. Thus $\mathcal F_\Sigma=\{0\}$: covariance has completely reversed the identity-covariance alignment.

Whitening does not resolve the problem. It transforms both the ordered-mean cone and coordinatewise lowerness into oblique cones, so the original order structure disappears.

## Recommended order of attack

1. Prove the low-cost $k$-dimensional sufficiency theorem.
2. Study necessity at $k=3$ and $\Sigma=I_3$, looking specifically for either a monotone coupling or a counterexample to the single-cut construction.
3. Study general $\Sigma$ first in the bivariate case, under transparent sign conditions such as $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1\ge0$.
4. Only then consider arbitrary $k$ and arbitrary $\Sigma$ simultaneously.

The main bottleneck for $k>2$ is multivariate transport. For general $\Sigma$, it is the possible incompatibility between the likelihood-ratio order and coordinatewise monotonicity.
