# Which Gaussian results use the unit diagonal?

**Comparison.** Keep the mean restriction in the units of observation, with every redundant mean bounded above by the largest core mean. Replace the correlation matrix by an arbitrary positive-definite covariance Σ. A positive diagonal change of units that also transforms the parameter space preserves the statistical decision problem. Changing Σ while imposing the original order in the new units poses a different question. A common marginal variance can always be removed by a common rescaling.

| Result in the notes | Dependence on marginal variances |
|---|---|
| Lemmas 1, 2 and 6, Definition 3, Propositions 4, 5 and 7 | The arguments carry over. Lemma 1 depends only on the mean space. The likelihood ratio, positivity argument, differentiation at zero and first-moment balance need positive definiteness. Use the feasible normals satisfying Σa ∈ Λ. |
| Corollary 8 | All nonnegative exact-size halfspaces remain admissible with two or more cores. Use the standard deviation of the linear statistic in its cutoff. The example following the corollary uses the unit diagonal to establish that a redundant coordinate normal is infeasible. |
| Corollary 9 and the unnumbered core-only rectangle corollary | Their automatic core-face feasibility uses common marginal variance. For each core face c, check Σ_{rc} ≤ max_{k core} Σ_{kc} for every redundant r. With multiple cores Proposition 7 can cover one infeasible essential face in total. Extra infeasible faces leave that argument inconclusive. With independent coordinates both core faces of the two-core example remain feasible at any positive marginal variances. |
| Corollary 10 | The continuity argument needs a strict margin at the core normal. A sufficient condition is Σ₁₁ > Σᵣ₁ for every redundant r. For a specified direction b, exact ties at zero can also be handled if (Σb)₁ ≥ (Σb)ᵣ at each tie. The unconditional conclusion can fail, as shown below. |
| Proposition 11 | The stated gate uses identity covariance. The strict dominance result extends to diagonal Σ = diag(vⱼ) by replacing X₁ + Xᵣ with X₁/v₁ + Xᵣ/vᵣ and recalibrating its cutoff. The pairwise log likelihood ratio, up to a constant, is μ₁(X₁/v₁ + Xᵣ/vᵣ) + (μ₁ − μᵣ)(−Xᵣ/vᵣ). Calibration gives equal null mass to the two exchanged regions and this score orders them strictly. Independence supplies the common factor from the other coordinates. |
| Exact bivariate characterization | For unit variances, all finite normals feasible is equivalent to 0 ≤ a₂ ≤ a₁ for finite normals of a closed convex lower acceptance region. For arbitrary Σ, even necessity of feasibility can fail with the same raw order. See below. |
| Singleton-core redundant-only test | Its universal inadmissibility in the lessons note uses the standardized bivariate theorem. For Σ with diagonal (5,1) and off-diagonal 2, Σe₂ = (2,1) is feasible, so Proposition 4 gives admissibility under the raw order. |

**One example checks Corollary 10 and the bivariate theorem.** Take one core, μ₂ ≤ μ₁, and Σ with diagonal (1,5) and off-diagonal 2. Its determinant is 1. For every ε > 0 and every alternative μ₁ > 0, the standardized mean of X₁ + εX₂ is at most (1 + ε)μ₁ / √(1 + 4ε + 5ε²), strictly below μ₁. The exact-size upper-tail test on X₁ strictly dominates the augmented test everywhere on the alternative.

The X₁ test itself is admissible. For the simple null mean (0,−1) and alternative mean (1,1), the difference is (1,2) = Σe₁. The X₁ test attains size α at that null and is the unique most powerful test for the pair. A competitor dominating it would have to coincide almost surely with it, hence at every mean by mutual absolute continuity. Yet its finite normal e₁ is infeasible at the origin because (1,2) violates the raw order.
