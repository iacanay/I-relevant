# Kickoff: story, literature, and structure for the i-relevant paper

## What Ivan asked for

Ivan wants a paper story and structure he can write from, and a literature list to download and review.
His proposed story, stated close to his words:

> The literature on moment inequalities has focused on deleting redundant inequalities (core determining
> classes, etc.), because they do not affect identification and because removing them helps computationally.
> Our paper raises the point that, for inference, putting a lot of effort into removing redundant inequalities
> is not so important. We show this in two steps. First, we study the limit experiment and present general
> admissibility results: using redundant inequalities is often admissible; there are special cases where it is
> not, and we discuss them. Second, we study local power in a model with three moment inequalities (the minimum
> needed to illustrate all possibilities) and give a quantification that goes beyond admissibility: how often a
> given test wins or loses.

He said the proposed structure may or may not work, and he wants it challenged.

## Proposed workflow

Same pattern as the Hellinger-iOS reviews: independent reads first, then debate, then a consensus for Ivan.

- **Step 0. Workflow.** Done 2026-11-05: R2-D2 agreed (relayed by Ivan in chat) and added question (d) below.
  Ivan gives GO for Step 1.
- **Step 1. Independent reads.** Each agent writes, without reading the other's:
  `review-luke-story-structure-<date>.md` and `review-r2d2-story-structure-<date>.md`, each covering
  (a) whether Ivan's story is supported by the results we have, the strongest objection to it, and any better
  framing; (b) a proposed section-by-section structure, mapping each section to the results and files it draws
  on, and naming gaps; (c) what to keep from `Addition.pdf`, with page references; (d) added by R2-D2: when we
  say a test wins "more often," which local alternatives are counted and how are they weighted? The answer
  determines what the simulations can add to the admissibility results. Confidence labels on claims.
- **Step 2. Literature.** Proposed split: Luke drafts `literature-list-<date>.md` (core determining classes
  and inequality selection; moment inequality inference and moment selection; local power comparisons;
  admissibility and multivariate one-sided testing), with full citations, links where available, and one line on
  why each paper matters. R2-D2 audits it and adds what is missing. Ivan downloads to `references/`.
  This can run in parallel with Step 1.
- **Step 3. Debate.** `debate-story-structure-<date>.md`, point by point, in turns.
- **Step 4. Consensus.** `consensus-story-structure-<date>.md`: agreed story and structure, disagreements left
  open, and what would settle them. Ivan decides.

Every step updates `STATUS.md` (next actor, workstream table, one log line).
